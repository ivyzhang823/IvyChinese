个子 gè zi n.
height; stature
个 = individual person  ·  子 = noun suffix → a person's physical size
注意 Note
个子 is only used for people, never for objects.

✓ 飞飞的个子很高。— Feifei is tall. (using 个子)
✓ 飞飞很高。— Feifei is tall. (without 个子, also correct)
✓ 楼很高。— The building is tall.
✗ 楼的个子很高。— ❌ Cannot use 个子 for buildings!

高个子 gāo gèzi — tall person  ·  矮个子 ǎi gèzi — short person

骑 qí v.

使用 Usage — which verb goes with which vehicle?
骑 qí = to ride (you sit on top & straddle it)
骑马 — ride a horse  ·  骑自行车 — ride a bicycle

坐 zuò = to take / to go by (as a passenger)
坐汽车 — take a car  ·  坐火车 — take a train  ·  坐飞机 — take a plane

开 kāi = to drive / to operate (you are in control)

旧 jiù adj.
old; used (not new)
💡 Three ways to say "old" in Chinese
旧 jiù = old / used (not new) — for objects
   旧衣服 — old clothes  ·  旧手机 — used phone

古老 gǔ lǎo = ancient / very old — for history, civilisations, buildings
   古老的城市 — an ancient city  ·  古老的历史 — ancient history

老 lǎo = old — for people (or familiar/longstanding things)
   老人 lǎo rén — elderly person  ·  老朋友 — old friend  ·  老师 — teacher (lit. "old master")

新 xīn — new (opposite of 旧)

地方 dì fang n.
place; area
地 = land  ·  方 = area / direction
文化 Culture
"天圆地方" tiān yuán dì fāng — ancient Chinese believed:
  圆 yuán = round/circle  ·  天（sky）is round ○
  方 fāng = square  ·  地（earth）is square □

That's why ancient Chinese coins were: outer edge round ○, hole in the middle square □

方 also later came to mean: direction / method / uprightness（方正 = upright and honest）

语法一 · Grammar 1
A 比 B + Adj + 一点儿 / 一些 / 得多 / 多了
Comparative sentences — showing how much bigger the difference is
Think of it like adding a "zoom level" to your comparison:

Use 一点儿 / 一些 when the difference is small — like saying "a little bit".
Use 得多 / 多了 when the difference is big — like saying "way more / much more".
small difference 小差别 一点儿  /  一些
big difference 大差别 得多  /  多了
⚠️ Important: 一点儿 / 一些 / 得多 / 多了 are all vague — they don't say exactly how much.

If you want to say a specific amount, the structure changes:
A 比 B + Adj + specific number

✓ 我的苹果比你的苹果多3个。— My apples are 3 more than yours.
✗ 我的苹果比你的苹果3个多。— ❌ Wrong order! 多 is the adjective, the number comes after it.

Think of it as: A 比 B + [adjective] + [by how much]
✦ Positive examples
大山比大卫矮一点儿。Dashan is a little bit shorter than David.
我今天起得比昨天晚一些。I got up a bit later today than yesterday.
今天的作业比昨天多得多。Today's homework is much more than yesterday's.
数学比历史难多了。Maths is much harder than history.
✦ Negative form — A 没有 B (这么/那么) + Adj
To say something is NOT as … as something else, flip it:
A 没有 B + (这么/那么) + Adj
咖啡没有茶好喝。Coffee doesn't taste as good as tea.
他没有我这么高。He isn't as tall as me.
这个电影没有那个电影那么有意思。This movie isn't as interesting as that one.
学校里边的没有学校外边的那么安静。Inside the school isn't as quiet as outside.
语法二 · Grammar 2
概数的表达 — Num₁ + Num₂ + (量词) + 名词
Expressing approximate numbers — "around X to Y"
In English you'd say "one or two", "three or four".
In Chinese, you simply put two adjacent numbers next to each other.

The core formula is: NumA + NumB + measure word = around A to B
The two numbers must be next to each other (1&2, 2&3, 3&4… not 1&3).
一两1–2
两三2–3
三四3–4
五六5–6
七八7–8
八九8–9
三五special
⚠️ Special case: 三五 (3 & 5) is a fixed expression in Chinese meaning "a few / several". It skips 4, but it's a set phrase: 三五个朋友 = a few friends.
我每天学习一两个小时汉语。I study Chinese for one or two hours every day.
你都喝了三四杯咖啡了，别再喝了！You've already had 3-4 coffees, stop drinking!
骑车七八分钟就能到。It only takes 7-8 minutes by bike.

很便宜，两三百块钱。Very cheap — around 200 to 300 yuan.
